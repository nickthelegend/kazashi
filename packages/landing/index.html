A shadow theatre for your room

Hold your hands to the light.

A paper lantern sits on your desk. Your hand's shadow falls on your real wall. Shape a dog, and the shadow inks into a living dog that trots along the wall and leaps the corner.

Meta Quest 3 · hands only · seated · WebXR

01 Three acts, fifteen shadows

02 The cast, on glass slides

Every creature is a hand pose authored in the emulator and run through the same shadow rasteriser as your live hands, so each one is reachable by construction. These silhouettes are drawn from that data.

03 How the shadow works

The lantern is a pinhole

Each frame, 25 tracked joints per hand become tapered capsules and a palm polygon, projected through the lantern onto your wall. A 64 × 64 raster, normalised for position and size, is scored by overlap against the target with ±10° of roll. No GPU readback.

Your room is the stage

Quest planes carry no labels, so Kazashi reads them by orientation, height and size: the desk for the lantern, the wall for the play, the ceiling for anything with wings. No wall? Pinch once to stand a shoji screen.

Hands, start to finish

Poke the paper tabs on the lantern stand. Turn your left palm up for the menu. Pinch a slide to cast it. Part your hands like a curtain and close them to record a 20-second play.

04 Run it

  1. Sit at a desk facing a wall, in a dim room if you can.
  2. Open the Kazashi link in the Meta Quest browser and tap Enter the theatre.
  3. Raise a flat hand over the lantern. Hold the shape until the ring fills.

Tested so far: the IWER emulator (Quest 3 and Meta VR Glasses profiles) with emulated hands. Not yet verified on a headset: tracking jitter on held poses, crossed-hand occlusion, thumb microgestures and 72 fps.
