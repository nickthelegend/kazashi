03 How the shadow works

The lantern is a pinhole

Each frame, 25 tracked joints per hand become tapered capsules and a palm polygon, projected through the lantern onto your wall. A 64 × 64 raster, normalised for position and size, is scored by overlap against the target with ±10° of roll. No GPU readback.

Your room is the stage

Quest planes carry no labels, so Kazashi reads them by orientation, height and size: the desk for the lantern, the wall for the play, the ceiling for anything with wings. No wall? Pinch once to stand a shoji screen.

Hands, start to finish

Poke the paper tabs on the lantern stand. Turn your left palm up for the menu. Pinch a slide to cast it. Part your hands like a curtain and close them to record a 20-second play.

05 Verification status & hardware limits

Verified in Emulator

  • IWER emulator runs on Quest 3 and Meta VR Glasses synthetic profiles.
  • Deterministic joint projection and capsule-to-wall shadow casting.
  • Automatic room plane classification (desk, wall, ceiling).
  • Act progression state machine and 20-second play recorder.
  • Zero console errors across end-to-end execution.

Pending Physical Quest Hardware

  • Real-camera optical tracking jitter on stationary held poses.
  • Crossed-hand visual self-occlusion during Act II two-handed figures.
  • Subtle thumb microgestures under rapid or low-contrast motion.
  • Sustained 72 fps display sync under adverse room lighting conditions.

06 How to run locally

  1. Sit at a desk facing a real wall, in a dim room if possible.
  2. Clone the repository and install dependencies: npm install && npm run dev.
  3. Open the local address in the Meta Quest Browser with WebXR hand tracking enabled.
  4. Tap Enter the theatre on the landing prompt.
  5. Raise a flat hand over the virtual lantern. Hold the shape until the ink ring fills.

Note: For remote Quest debugging, use adb reverse tcp:8094 tcp:8094 over USB or connect via your local Wi-Fi subnet.
